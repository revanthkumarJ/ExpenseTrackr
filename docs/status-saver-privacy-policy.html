Status Saver — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026
In short: Status Saver has no accounts, no sign-up and no in-app purchases or subscriptions. Your statuses, the files you save, the files you hide in the vault and the numbers you type into Direct chat never leave your phone. The app sends anonymous diagnostics through Google Firebase so bugs can be found and fixed, and — from the second week after you install it — shows ads from Google AdMob, which uses your device's advertising ID. Neither ever receives your file names, file contents or phone numbers.

This Privacy Policy explains how the Status Saver Android application ("Status Saver", "the app", "we", "us") handles your information. By using the app, you agree to the practices described here.

1. What we do not collect

We have no user accounts and no sign-up. We do not collect, store or transmit your name, email address, contacts, precise location or payment details. We never access, upload or share the statuses you view, the files you save or the files you hide in the vault — those stay on your device, and there is no server of ours for them to go to.

The app has no in-app purchases and no subscriptions; every feature is free, so no payment or purchase information is processed at all.

2. What the app does send

Status Saver uses two Google Firebase services — Firebase Crashlytics and Google Analytics for Firebase — and, after the first week, Google AdMob for ads (section 3). The Firebase services are anonymous and exist for one reason: Android storage behaves differently on nearly every phone model, and without diagnostics a bug that only happens on one manufacturer's device is invisible to us.

Firebase Crashlytics (crash reports)

When the app crashes, or when it fails to read, save, edit or move a file and recovers from it, Crashlytics sends a report containing the technical stack trace, your device model, Android version, the app version and the time it happened, along with a randomly generated installation identifier that is not linked to you and is reset if you reinstall the app. Recovered failures are labelled with a fixed internal name such as save_to_downloads — never with the name of the file involved.

Google Analytics for Firebase (usage counts)

Analytics counts how the app is used, so we can tell which features are worth keeping and where people get stuck. It records which screen is open and a fixed list of actions, for example:

  • saving, cropping, trimming, sharing, setting as wallpaper, reposting or deleting a status, and whether it succeeded;
  • granting folder access or picking the wrong folder, and opening WhatsApp;
  • using a tool (video to GIF, compress, remove sound, change speed, collage);
  • hiding, restoring or deleting files in the vault, and a failed unlock;
  • opening Direct chat, or entering a number that cannot be valid;
  • changing theme, accent colour, style, grid size, language or save location;
  • searching, sorting or starring downloads; placing the home-screen widget; using a launcher shortcut or the Quick Settings tile;
  • seeing the walkthrough, what's new or an update prompt; and tapping "More apps", "Rate", "Share app" or the privacy link.

Each is recorded as an event name with, at most, short fixed labels such as the media type ("image" or "video"), the screen it came from, the tool or theme chosen, or a technical error code. Analytics also records standard device information — model, Android version, app version, language and approximate country — session counts, and a random app-instance identifier that Firebase generates for this installation.

Never included, on either Firebase service: file names, file contents, thumbnails, folder paths, the statuses you viewed or saved, who posted them, phone numbers or messages typed into Direct chat, your contacts, your precise location, your advertising ID, or any account or contact detail. Analytics' own advertising-ID collection and ad-personalisation signals are switched off, so Firebase never links your usage to an ad profile. We do not sell data to anyone.

3. Advertising (Google AdMob)

For the first 7 days after you install Status Saver there are no ads at all — the ad software is not even started. After that, the app shows a small number of ads from Google AdMob: a banner at the bottom of some secondary screens (such as Settings and Tools), an occasional ad tile in Downloads and between statuses in the full-screen viewer, and at most one full-screen ad after finishing an edit or tool, no more often than every few minutes. There are never ads on the Statuses screen, in the vault, or while you save or play a status.

To show and measure ads and to prevent ad fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects and shares with Google: your device's advertising ID and app set ID, your IP address (which may be used to estimate your general location), interactions with ads (such as ad views and taps) and diagnostic information about app and ad performance. This is sent encrypted. AdMob never receives your statuses, saved files, file names or anything you type into Direct chat.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app asks for your consent through Google's consent form before personalised ads are shown, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Ad privacy choices. Anywhere, you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of ad personalisation, in your phone's settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads on most Android phones). How Google uses this information is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

4. Features that handle your data on your device only

  • Direct chat: the number and optional message you type are passed straight to WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business to open the chat. They are never stored by the app and never sent to us.
  • Vault: files you hide are moved into the app's private storage on your phone and are excluded from cloud backups. The vault is opened with your phone's own screen lock (fingerprint, face, PIN or pattern) through Android's system prompt — the app never sees or stores your fingerprint, face data or PIN. Files in the vault are deleted if you uninstall the app, so restore anything you want to keep first.
  • Editing and tools: cropping, trimming, saving a frame or the audio, GIFs, compression, speed changes and collages all run on your phone. Temporary working copies live in the app's cache and are deleted once the result is saved.
  • Home-screen widget and Quick Settings tile: show how many statuses are waiting, counted on your phone.

5. Permissions we request and why

  • Internet (INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE): used to deliver the anonymous diagnostics described above and, after the first week, to load ads. No app feature needs the network — browsing, saving, editing and sharing all work fully offline, and an ad space with no connection simply stays empty.
  • Access to the WhatsApp statuses folder: on Android 10 and newer, the app asks you once to select WhatsApp's .Statuses folder using Android's own folder picker. The app reads that folder to list the statuses you have already viewed. The permission stays on your device and can be revoked at any time in Android settings.
  • Storage (READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, on Android 9 and older only): to read the statuses folder and to copy the files you choose into your Downloads or Pictures folder.
  • Biometric / screen lock (USE_BIOMETRIC, USE_FINGERPRINT): to show Android's own unlock prompt for the vault. Biometric data never leaves Android's secure hardware.
  • App visibility (queries): so the app can tell whether WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business is installed, open it, start a Direct chat, and send a status back to it when you tap "Repost".
  • Wake lock (WAKE_LOCK): used by the bundled video player and video-processing components so the screen and processing behave correctly.
  • Advertising ID (AD_ID, plus Android's ad-services permissions): used by Google AdMob to show and measure ads, as described in section 3.
  • Install referrer (BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE): added by the Firebase library so Google Play can tell Analytics that an install happened. It reads nothing from your device and is not used for advertising.

These permissions are used solely to make the app work and for no other purpose.

6. Where your files are stored

Statuses you save are copied into public folders on your own device — Download/StatusSaver/WhatsApp, or Pictures/StatusSaver/WhatsApp if you choose Gallery under Settings → Save to. Audio you save from a video goes to Music/StatusSaver. These files belong to you: they are visible in your gallery and file manager, and they remain on your device even if you uninstall Status Saver.

The app also keeps a small local preferences file holding your settings (theme, language, save location, the folder you selected), your favourites, the names of statuses already seen (for the NEW badge) and simple counters shown on the "Your stats" screen. It never leaves your phone, and uninstalling the app deletes it.

7. Sharing and selling data

We do not sell or trade any data, and we never share your files. The anonymous diagnostics described in section 2 are processed on our behalf by Google as part of the Firebase platform, and the advertising data described in section 3 is shared with Google AdMob, under Firebase's privacy terms and Google's Privacy Policy. They are visible only to the app's developer, as aggregate counts and crash reports.

When you tap "Share" or "Repost", Android hands the selected file to the app you pick (for example WhatsApp); from that point the receiving app's own privacy policy applies. When you tap "More apps", "Rate", a privacy link or an update prompt, Android opens the Google Play Store or your browser, which are governed by Google's Privacy Policy. Google Play's in-app review and in-app update screens are shown by the Play Store app itself.

8. Data retention

We hold no data ourselves and run no server. The diagnostics sit in Google's Firebase systems under Google's retention settings: Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days, and user-level Analytics data expires on a rolling window (two months by default), after which only aggregated, non-identifying counts remain. Files you save, files in the vault and the app's local preferences stay on your device until you delete them or uninstall the app.

9. Your choices

Because the diagnostics are anonymous and carry no account, there is nothing tied to you for us to look up, export or delete. For ads, use Settings → Ad privacy choices (where offered) and your phone's ad settings, as described in section 3. The app does not currently offer an in-app switch for them; uninstalling Status Saver stops all collection immediately, and Android's per-app network settings can also block it. If you have a question or a request about anything described here, email us at the address in section 14 and we will answer.

10. Children's privacy

Status Saver is a general-audience utility and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.

11. Security

Your statuses and saved files never leave your device, so there is no account to breach and no server of ours holding your content. Vault files sit in the app's private storage and open only after your phone's screen lock. The anonymous diagnostics are sent to Google over an encrypted connection.

12. Content ownership

Status Saver does not download, host, or distribute any content itself. It only gives you access to media that WhatsApp has already stored on your device after you viewed it. Statuses belong to the people who posted them. Please respect their rights and obtain permission before re-sharing or re-posting someone else's content.
